## Two corrections to v15/v16/v17

1. **The drain pad was in the wrong size class.** The Telegram quickjs-ng
   fork (ENABLE_DUMPS off) allocates `"x".repeat(L)` as `L + 33` bytes:
   `repeat(960)` = 993 → Apple tiny class **1008**, not 976. Every v15/v16/v17
   string-pad variant (`P=768/1536/2304/4300`, `padfirst`, `padafter`,
   `reorder`, `padstr`) therefore drained **nothing**: measured with the
   device-fork engine + `tiny_emu15`, `v17_pl_padfirst.js` makes only 3,001
   class-976 allocations (3,000 spray + 1 `b`; the 1,024-string pad = 0).
   The v17 "page-side placement exhausted" sweep only exercised the view
   spray and the hole position, not the drain.
   The correct class-976 pad is **`"x".repeat(936)`** = 969 bytes.
2. **The 8 MiB heap cap is per widget runtime, not per process.** A widget
   bubble is one `MessageWidgetInstance`/`WRSession`/`JSRuntime` with its own
   heap; a runtime lives as long as its bubble. N live bubbles hold N×K
   class-976 blocks. The single-runtime pages of v15/v16/v17 were capped at
   ~7.2k blocks; the 5725 open had at least the 16:22 payload bubble live and
   holding ~3.1k blocks above the 5725 message (`v2/_proof_alive.png`), with
   the 16:25/16:26 bubbles as likely further holds.

The v17 "the free list never hands the page two blocks 1,343 apart" is true
for one chosen model structure (`TINY_FREED_FRAC=10`) and one runtime; it is
not a device statement. The pair is not found in the free list — it is
manufactured by exhausting the list, after which the payload's own fresh
tiny-zone regions are address-contiguous.
